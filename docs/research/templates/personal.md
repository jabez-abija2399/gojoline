# Personal site (portfolio / CV / freelancer) — design brief

## Sites opened and what I took
- **brittanychiang.com** — Name, role ("Frontend Engineer"), one-sentence promise ("I build accessible, pixel-perfect experiences for the web"). Order: About → Experience → Projects → Writing. Experience rows = date range, role + company, 2-line description, skill tags. Project cards carry a proof badge ("100k+ Installs"). One typeface (Inter). CTA "View Full Résumé".
- **lucaslima.design** (Awwwards portfolio list) — Huge "Senior Product Designer" display heading, short bio, email with copy button, expertise chips, persistent "Open to work" link in the header, EN/PT language toggle. "Selected work" cards: thumbnail, title, one-line *outcome*, category tag, "View ↗". Numbered lists (01–04) for side projects and writing.
- **zenobasi.com** (Awwwards) — Name + role as the whole hero ("Zen Obasi, Art Director"), "Folio No. 01" label, availability line "Accepting Briefs for S/S 2027", one CTA everywhere: "Inquire".
- **vanschneider.com** — All-caps statement hero ("I CREATE; THEREFORE I AM"), eyebrow "EST. 2000", portrait captioned with name + role. Projects: discipline tags → "Client — one-line value" → short paragraph → "View project".
- **jessicahische.is** — Role sentence as intro ("I'm a lettering artist, author… based in Oakland"). Featured projects alternate large image + "Client: X" + "View Project". Logo grid ("Selected"). Four closing CTA cards incl. "Let's Make Something". "Work with me" split by service (commissions, coaching, speaking).
- **rauno.me** — Extreme minimal: name, role, a short stacked manifesto, an Email link that copies the address.
- **awwwards.com/websites/portfolio** and **Framer portfolio templates** — category pitch: "put your best work first… case studies, experience, background". Winners are mostly type-led, near-monochrome.
- Round-ups (weblium, search summary): best sites make three things clear in seconds — what you do, proof, how to hire you.

## Hero pattern
Portrait is optional; **type is the hero**. Small eyebrow (location + availability chip with a green dot: "Available for work · Addis Ababa"), then a huge name or role statement (2–4 lines), a one-sentence promise, two buttons: primary "Hire me / Get in touch" (mailto, Telegram or WhatsApp), secondary "View work" or "Download CV". Portrait sits beside or below as a 4:5 crop with rounded corners, not full-bleed.

## Home page order
1. Header: name wordmark, 3–4 anchors, language toggle, contact button
2. Hero (above)
3. Selected work: 4–6 cards
4. About: portrait + 3 short paragraphs + skill chips
5. Services / what I do (3–4 rows, optional "from" price)
6. Experience timeline (and education)
7. Testimonials or client names (text, not logos if none)
8. Contact block: big line ("Let's work together"), email, phone, socials
9. Footer: one line

## Signature UI
- Oversized name/role statement
- "Available for work" chip with pulsing dot
- Work cards: image, **project title**, client/type tag, year, one-line outcome
- Numbered rows (01, 02…) with thin divider lines
- Experience timeline: `2022 — Present · Role · Company`, tags below
- Email with copy affordance; arrows "↗" on outbound links
- Stat strip is rare; if used: years, projects, clients

## Typography
Two workable characters: (a) neutral grotesk (Inter, Geist, Manrope) at very large size, tight tracking (-0.02 to -0.04em), sentence case; (b) editorial serif display (Fraunces, Instrument Serif) for the statement + sans body. Labels: 11–12px uppercase, +0.08em tracking, often monospace. Hero 44–56px at 360px wide, 96–140px desktop. Amharic: no negative tracking, no uppercase, line-height ≥1.35 (Noto Sans/Serif Ethiopic).

## Palettes
- **Ink + Marigold (warm, designer)**: primary `#E8590C`, dark accent `#17130F`, light background `#FAF6EF`, text `#1F1B16`
- **Midnight + Highland green (dev/consultant)**: primary `#0F766E`, dark accent `#0B1F2A`, light background `#F4F7F5`, text `#14212B`

## Photo style
One confident portrait, plain or single-colour wall, natural or soft studio light, direct gaze, chest-up, vertical 4:5. A second looser portrait with empty space for the About section. Work samples shot as objects (brochure on wood, laptop on desk, logo on tablet) in consistent 4:3 or 3:2 crops; editorial photos full-bleed. No stock "team" shots.

## What converts on mobile
- Contact button fixed in header; tap-to-call, WhatsApp/Telegram deep links before any form
- Work cards one per row, full width, title visible without tapping
- CV as a small PDF link; whole page under ~300 KB of images above the fold
- Availability chip + city in the first screen

## Do
1. Say the role and city in the first 5 words of the hero.
2. Give every work card a real title, type and one outcome line.
3. Show availability and a single contact route on every screen.
4. Keep to one accent colour and lots of empty space.
5. Use a dated experience timeline — it doubles as the CV.

## Don't
1. No skill-percentage bars or star ratings of yourself.
2. No carousel hero or auto-playing animation that hides the work.
3. No untitled image grids — a picture without a project name proves nothing.
